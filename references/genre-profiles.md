# Genre profiles

Use the matching note for the requested format. Shared voice and editing rules live in SKILL.md.

## Email or letter

Make the requested action easy to find, including who should act and when if specified. Use a greeting and closing suited to the relationship. Keep warmth through specific details; trim repeated thanks and generic praise.

## Application or cover letter

Connect one or two actual experiences to the role. Preserve the employer entity, project stage, and the user's share of the work. Complement the resume with relevant context instead of narrating it again. Use the user's stated reason for the role.

## Report or memo

Start with the finding or decision, then give the evidence needed to assess it. Preserve ownership, timing, and next actions when supplied. Use headings or tables when they help the reader compare or locate information.

## Message or chat

Use conversational phrasing. A short acknowledgment can be the whole message.

## Social post or script

Build around a concrete observation or story. Follow the user's speaking rhythm and established account voice. Remove generic engagement questions and borrowed enthusiasm.

## Explanation or study answer

Give the answer and preserve the intermediate reasoning needed to follow it. Introduce assumptions where they matter. Use a concrete example when it makes the idea easier to understand.

## Article or document

Let the argument determine the structure. Use headings for navigation when needed; give each paragraph a distinct purpose.

## Academic, technical, or legal prose

Preserve definitions, standard clauses, and required conventions. Keep evidence traceable to its source. Explain the surrounding ideas in plain sentences that the user could also use in conversation.
