# English examples

These examples show the intended voice. Apply the shared rules in SKILL.md.

| Overwritten | Natural |
| --- | --- |
| I am reaching out to inquire about the possibility of arranging a meeting. | Could we meet? |
| This experience provided me with valuable insights into how teams collaborate. | I learned how teams work together. |
| My objective is to investigate the implications of uncertainty in probability estimates for optimal betting decisions. | I want to study how uncertainty in estimated probabilities affects how much to bet. |
| Due to the fact that the sample size was limited, the findings may not be generalizable. | The sample was small, so the results may not apply more broadly. |
| I would like to emphasize that this is merely a preliminary suggestion and should not be interpreted as a definitive recommendation. | This is an initial suggestion. |

## Sentence pattern examples

| Pattern | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decorative rule of three | I want to grow, develop, and reach my full potential. | I want to improve. |
| Repeated parallel sentences | I checked the data. I checked the code. I checked the results. | I checked the data, code, and results. |
| Manufactured contrast | This is not just useful. It is essential. | This is essential. |
| Staged reveal | The result? We finished on time. | We finished on time. |
| Comma insertion | This method, in my view, is easier to use. | I think this method is easier to use. |
| Dash insertion | The sample was small—but still useful. | The sample was small but still useful. |
| Nouns hiding an action | We conducted an analysis of the data. | We analyzed the data. |

A factual list such as `The model uses age, income, and debt` keeps all three inputs. Commas separating list items are not a paired comma insertion. In the checking example, adding `then` would invent an order of events.

## Other forms to recognize

| Family | Examples to review |
| --- | --- |
| Manufactured contrast | `less about X, more about Y`; `not because X, but because Y`; `No X. No Y. Just Z.`; `The challenge was not X. It was Y.` |
| Decorative coverage | `whether X or Y`; `across X, Y, and Z` without a meaningful set or comparison |
| Stock enthusiasm | `I am excited to bring my A, B, and C`; generic praise transferable to any applicant or company |
| Inflated importance | `marks a pivotal moment`; `at its core`; unsupported `transformative` or `groundbreaking` |
| Empty consequence | `highlighting its profound impact`; `ensuring success` without evidence for that conclusion |

## Meaning checks in practice

`Estimated annual savings of 120 hours` can become `We expect to save 120 hours a year` when the source identifies the writer as the estimator. It still describes an estimate, not savings already achieved.

`The changes happened together` does not establish that one caused the other. A connector such as `so` adds a causal claim; choose it from the meaning rather than for smoother flow.

`Experts agree` needs an identifiable source. Replacing it with `research shows` leaves the information gap unresolved. Preserve an acronym when its expansion is unknown.

Keep established technical uses such as `robust standard errors`; simplify the surrounding explanation.

Use ordinary sentence structure to remove an insertion. Replacing every dash with a semicolon leaves the same awkward structure in place.

## Question and answer example

**Source**

Question: What motivated you to pursue this opportunity?

Answer: My motivation stems from a strong desire to further enhance my understanding of how quantitative methods are applied in practical settings.

**Edited**

Question: What motivated you to pursue this opportunity?

Answer: I want to learn how quantitative methods are used in practice.
